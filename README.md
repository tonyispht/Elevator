# Elevator

A first-person browser game set inside a low-poly elevator. Ride between floors P and 1–100 (arcade, pool, bowling, ballroom, sky deck, tea house, orbital station, chess club, Roman baths, mission control, chalet, science lab, jungle temple, bank vault, Moroccan riad, cloud lounge, planetarium, pirate cove, haunted manor, submarine, circus, crystal cavern, airship, throne room, time lab, starship bridge, a rooftop summit on floor 100, and four huge residences), flipping through the paged button panel, explore the lobbies, the parking garage, an abandoned metro station (with a Dr. Jessie Benson mural), and find the keys to the apartments.

Play: https://tonyispht.github.io/Elevator/

Optional jobs (open the Job Board with the 💼 button or J; take one or quit any time, pay goes into a saved $ wallet):
- **Courier** – carry packages to a floor and walk onto the glowing ring; faster deliveries earn tips and streak bonuses.
- **Elevator operator** – guests wait on floors and call the lift; pick them up, drop them at their floor, build combos. Some guests are **riddle passengers**: they describe their destination instead of naming it ("Sand, surf and a striped umbrella"), they only ever ask for floors you have already visited, and a correct first guess pays a bonus.
- **Tour guide** – lead a 3-stop tour and hold at each spot's star ring for a few seconds; faster legs earn more stars.
- **Photographer** – clients ask for a photo of a particular floor's secret; open photo mode (P), centre the subject and shoot. Better framing pays more and streaks add a bonus.

Floor secrets: every floor (P and 1–100) hides one interactive thing that belongs only to that floor – a cuckoo clock, a Tesla coil, a vault, a time machine, a telescope, a summit flag on floor 100… Look for the little gold sparkle, then click or press E. Your finds are saved ("✨ New find").

Journal and the vault: press **K** (or the 📖 button) for the Journal. It tracks every floor secret, night find, rat hole, metro find and the building's strange "signal". Each 20 finds reveal one digit of a four-digit code; the keypad on the vault door on floor 100 opens a gold reward and a skin for the lift panel.

The signal: as you go higher the tower glitches more, with static, flickering and intercom fragments that slowly tell a story. Collected fragments are kept in the Journal's Signal tab.

Shop (from the Job Board, or the Journal): pet treats, an express lift motor, an emergency lantern for blackouts, a zoom lens for photo mode, lift music packs (bossa, synth), and brass and neon panel skins.

Photo mode: press **P**, aim at a floor secret, night find, pet or the metro train, and click or press E. Your best shot of each subject is kept in the Journal's Album, where you can save the last photo as an image. Scroll or +/− zooms.

Tower Sprint: in the Journal's Sprint tab, race to floors 20, 40, 60, 80 and 100 in order. The clock starts when you press the first floor; a wrong floor costs 2 seconds; the lift sometimes jams, so mash the ALARM button to free it. Your best time and splits are saved.

Blackouts and stalls: now and then the lift jams between floors (mash ALARM), or the whole tower loses power. During a blackout the lift refuses to move; find the blinking red breaker box in the lobby and flip it for a tip.

Night finds: six extra secrets (a shooting star, an owl, fireflies, a moon lantern, a constellation, a sleeping cat) appear only after dark by your real clock.

Pets: give Hopper, the metro chihuahua, a treat (and leave one in the bowl for a rat) and they follow you through the tower. A rat can squeeze into the twelve tiny holes hidden in lobby walls and come back with a stash.

Dog park and adoption: outside the parking garage there is a **Dog Park** gate in the city. Behind it is a fenced yard where seven dogs (two puppies, a Jack Russell, a Bull Terrier, a Basset Hound, an Alaskan Malamute and a Bullmastiff) trot around, chase each other, sit, scratch and lie down. Pick up the tennis ball by the gate basket and throw it: the nearest dogs race after it and bring it back. Walk up to any dog to meet it and **adopt** it (free). Up to three adopted dogs follow you through the tower at once; manage who comes along in the Journal's **Pack** tab or at the adoption desk. Adopted dogs appear as photo subjects too.

Jail: a few things a sensible person would not do get you arrested, booked, and put in a cell: hammering the HELP or ALARM button in the cab, pulling a fire alarm with no fire (pull stations on a few floors, e.g. 1, 4, 9, 17, 25, 31, 50 and 77), trying a stranger's car door in the garage, climbing the fence by a NO EXIT sign in the city, or poking a breaker box when nothing is wrong. After a booking slip you wake up in a rusty cell with a flickering bulb, a toilet, a sink, a tally on the wall and a rules board. The door is locked; the only way out is to sleep in the bed, which puts you back in the lift on floor 1. If you reload while jailed you are still in the cell.

Laundry day: your clothes get dirtier the longer you play. After about 45 minutes of play you start to smell (a chip shows how bad it is), after about 90 you are ripe (a green haze and the odd fly), and after about 150 you are a hazard (flies everywhere, people and plants react). The Laundromat on floor 84 takes $4 and a 45-second wash-and-dry cycle, which you spend waiting in a towel; collect your clothes from the machine you used and the clock resets. The state is saved, so you cannot dodge it by reloading.

Cars and the drive to town: three of the ten cars in the garage are for sale (a cyan microcar for $600, a green pickup for $1,500, a red supercar for $3,800); walk up to one to see the price and buy it, with a 60% trade-in on any car you already own. With the keys you can choose "Drive somewhere" from your own car: a short night-drive cutscene (tap to skip; the supercar gets there fastest) takes you to the Big Basket Market, a full grocery store (produce, deli counter, freezers, snack and pet aisles, checkout lanes), or the Galleria Mall (a wishing fountain, Threads clothing, Paw Palace, Sweet Tooth, a food court and an arcade). "Drive home" parks you back beside your car. You also get hungry: peckish after about 50 minutes without eating, hungry after 100, starving after 160 (a chip, stomach growls and a darker screen at the end). Food from the store or the food court fixes it, candy, fizzy drinks and iced coffee give a short speed boost, Threads sells a fresh outfit (clears any smell) and cologne (masks it for 20 minutes), and Paw Palace sells pet treats cheaper than the Journal. All of it is procedural, so there are no new model credits.

Homes: buy any apartment from the Journal's 🏠 Homes tab (3B $900, 3C $1,400, 3D $700, 3E $2,600; the four floor-24 residences $7,500 to $10,000) and you get its key straight away. Buying clears the flat of its original furniture (kitchens and bathrooms keep their fittings; 3B's furniture is baked into the model so it stays and you only add to it). Inside, the 🏠 Home button (or H) opens the shop, your storage, a Style tab and a Home tab. The shop sells 28 pieces (beds, sofas, chairs, tables, a bookcase, a TV unit, a coffee machine, plants, rugs and more), all taken from the apartments' own models so they work in every home, plus the ASUS ROG Zephyrus Duo 16 laptop. Buying puts a see-through ghost in your hands: look where you want it (green fits, red does not), R turns it 90 degrees, the mouse wheel 15, and Enter or click places it. Small things like laptops, books and plants can sit on tables and desks. Click a placed piece to pick it up and move it, or store it. Big furniture blocks the player. The Style tab changes wall and floor colours and patterns (stripes, pinstripes, dots, subway tile, chevron, panelling, damask; planks, tiles, checker, herringbone, carpet, concrete). Everything is saved. You can switch back to the apartment's original furniture at any time without losing your own pieces.

Metro stations: when the train rests at the platform, board it to ride to three other stops (Night Market, Rail Depot, Harbour Quay), each with its own props and secrets. The red button post at each platform calls the train home.

Holiday decor appears in the lift from December 1 to January 2.

Light switches: every room has a switch plate (by the lift door, or beside the exit door in apartments, the metro, the bar and the warehouse) that turns the lights off and on. A few places have flaky wiring and glitch off and on by themselves.

Metro: a train thunders in at speed, brakes, rests at the platform for a few seconds and pulls out again; every other run is an express that blasts straight through.

Controls: WASD / drag to look / click or E to use / Shift to run / J jobs / K journal / P photo mode / M mute. Touch: left stick to move (push fully to run), drag to look, USE button.
